**Assessment convention**

Use Admiralty-style source reliability A–F alongside information credibility 1–6. A–E assess a source from reliable through unreliable; F means reliability cannot be judged. Credibility runs from 1, independently confirmed, through 2 probably true, 3 possibly true, 4 doubtful, and 5 improbable; 6 means it cannot be judged. This is a two-part assessment, not one probability. F and 6 are unassessed states and must not be averaged into a risk score. The military convention and its interpretation are discussed in [Icard, 2024, §2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.19968v1#S2.SS1).

For this initial audit:
- F2 is a provisional assessment of directly retrieved, internally consistent source or account observations. Source reliability history was not independently established.
- F6 marks live state, completeness, mappings, or outcomes that were not established.
- A repository's assertion is evidence that the repository makes that assertion. It does not establish the asserted runtime or scientific outcome.
- Your instructions are requirements, not uncertain factual claims to be scored.
